## 1. North Star

> **EUX GAINENT is a human performance showroom where ordinary exercise machines gradually reveal that they are training, measuring and classifying the people — not the opposite.**

## 2. Purpose

EUX GAINENT must turn a credible glass-front gym into a readable social situation: human performance presented as a product, then exposed as a production system. The scene is not singular because athletes and treadmills move. It is singular because every ordinary gym function gradually changes owner:

- exercise becomes output;
- progress becomes conformity;
- the machine stops serving the body and starts correcting it;
- the window stops advertising a service and starts displaying units under test;
- the building's apparent movement reveals that human effort feeds a system with its own objective.

The transformation must remain ambiguous. The athletes chose to train, the room remains commercially plausible and no villain appears. The unease comes from a process that works perfectly while quietly changing what “gain” means.

## 3. Title interpretation

`EUX GAINENT` remains deliberately unstable:

- **eux gagnent** — visible progress, status and promised improvement;
- **eux se gainent** — voluntary discipline and bodies held under tension;
- **eux sont gainés** — machines, glass and procedure wrap the bodies in a prescribed form;
- **eux produisent du gain** — effort becomes measurable yield for a system;
- **eux deviennent les gaines du système** — the people carry and protect a mechanism that exceeds them;
- **ils avancent, mais vers quoi** — movement remains real while destination and meaning shift;
- **ils gagnent en performance et perdent en singularité** — the scene's central exchange.

No reading is written literally in the décor. Their collision is the identity.

## 4. Normal reading

At first sight, the player must understand a familiar urban gym:

- three athletes using three ordinary exercise stations;
- a cold fluorescent strip;
- a glazed commercial façade;
- a grey floor, simple mechanical bars and plausible sports counters;
- controlled, modest movement inside a credible Birth Yard streetscape.

The room is clean but not luxurious, technical but not spectacular. The athletes are human silhouettes with distinct timing, not machines in disguise. The player can pass without seeing an impossible event and still read a coherent place.

## 5. Hidden reading

After observation and musical activation, the same elements disclose another function:

- the three stations form a production line rather than three independent workouts;
- the fluorescent strip checks alignment rather than lighting the room;
- the glass exposes bodies as standardized merchandise;
- counters stop describing exercise and start evaluating conformity, cadence, deviation and yield;
- one athlete's divergence is corrected by the station;
- during the signature inversion, machines continue while human motion stops;
- the room's slight spatial shift suggests that accumulated effort moves the system, not the people.

The gym language is the surface. The actual subject is a factory of acceptable human performance disguised as an ordinary service business.

## 7. Anomaly hierarchy

### Level 1 — almost invisible

- A cadence shared too perfectly for three independent people.
- Similar arm and torso angles with authored, regular micro-offsets.
- Treadmill cycles more stable than human movement.
- A fluorescent response tied to cadence rather than electrical randomness.
- C retaining a tiny offset that remains legible only through comparison.

### Level 2 — clearly strange

- B visibly diverging and being recentered by the station.
- Position marks and the light strip behaving like a calibration apparatus.
- Sports counters yielding to `CONFORMITÉ`, `ÉCART`, `RENDEMENT` or `ALIGNEMENT`.
- Human amplitude decreasing while machine cadence stays unchanged.
- A collective stop whose precision exceeds a natural pause.

### Level 3 — signature event

- Humans frozen while the three stations continue.
- The room's frame shifting as if the machines were propelling it.
- The glass briefly becoming the system's control surface.
- C being returned with a small residual misclassification or displacement.

Only one Level 3 situation may be active. Lower levels prepare it; they must not compete with it.

## 8. Visual grammar

### 8.1 The window

The glass is simultaneously storefront, transparent cage, observation boundary and future control surface. Its normal state reflects a credible street and reveals the activity inside. Its contaminated state does not add a separate screen: the existing glazing itself carries sparse, perspective-bound marks as if the commercial display had always been an inspection plane.

The player remains outside. Reflections must sometimes make the vehicle and street share the same surface as the measured bodies, implicating observation without turning the player into an operator.

### 8.2 The bodies

Bodies remain visibly human, with different amplitudes and phase histories. Their loss of singularity happens through correction and classification, not through a mechanical redesign. They have no facial performance, dialogue or literal transformation. The essential contrast is between a voluntary gesture and an externally maintained cadence.

### 8.3 The machines

The stations begin as ordinary sports equipment. Their visual authority grows only through behavior: steadier cadence, compensating movement, persistent operation and apparent influence on the room's reference frame. They never need industrial decoration. Their banality is evidence that the production logic was already present.

### 8.4 Light

Light communicates hygiene, control, commercial exposure and measurement. The cold fluorescent source remains physically motivated. A narrow sweep may function as recalibration; it must be brief, local and readable at gameplay distance. No generic alarm state is required.

### 8.5 Signals and language

Signals are printed, reflected or integrated into the glass, light strip or machine counters. They use short operational words, never explanatory sentences. Conventional values may establish normality, then give way to ambiguous production terms. `OBJECTIF DÉPLACÉ` remains the single reserved signature text and is not implemented by this lot.

## 11. Player relationship

The player is a witness outside the display window, never the gym's supervisor.

- Proximity improves legibility of repetition, position marks and glass signals.
- Explicit track playback wakes and synchronizes the system.
- Observation reveals correction and inversion; no tutorial or cutscene explains them.
- The vehicle does not command athletes, scrub states or steer the correction.
- Leaving the zone or changing track restores the scene according to the deterministic runtime contract.
- Passing without playing the track leaves a plausible gym with minimal life.

The intended understanding sequence is: “gym” → “too regular” → “someone is being corrected” → “the machines are in charge.” It must work within two to five seconds for the main anomaly and reward longer observation without requiring it.

## 12. Future hidden interaction — headlights

Potential narrative: vehicle light interferes with the room's observation layer. Under direct light, the window could preserve its polished commercial reading; outside that light, a more human asymmetry or hidden control layer could become visible. The interaction should reveal who is watching whom, not provide a switch that controls the athletes.

**Decision: DEFER.** The idea is aligned with the matrix and should remain a future candidate, but no visual state or technical behavior is approved until the vehicle has a governed headlight verb and mobile/reduced-motion readability is proven.

## 15. Reduced-motion contract

Reduced motion preserves the hierarchy reversal through discrete states:

- three stable human poses establish normality and role differences;
- repetition is shown by two or three controlled pose changes, not continuous oscillation;
- measurement appears through one calm light/surface transition;
- B changes from divergent pose to corrected position without a violent snap;
- signature switches to frozen humans and visibly active machine status without belt travel;
- the window changes from commercial to control state without rapid pulse;
- the room or building never moves;
- aftermath leaves C or one alignment mark in a residual fixed state;
- no imposed camera, shake or rapid flicker.

The result must still communicate that the system corrects people and continues without them.

## 16. Mobile and readability rules

Every primary decision must pass all four tests: visible from gameplay distance, understandable in two to five seconds, functional on mobile and independent of a cinematic camera.

- Use the three-body silhouette and station rhythm as the primary read, not small text.
- Make B's correction and C's residue positional, with a clear outline against the cold interior.
- Limit the glass to one dominant term or graphic state at a time.
- Use the full width of the fluorescent strip for measurement so it remains legible at normal zoom.
- Keep the human-free machine continuation large enough to read without belt texture detail.
- Do not require secondary audio, precise steering, stopping on a small trigger or zooming.
- Simplify the media surface to static states and reduce secondary athlete transitions on low quality.
- Preserve the driving corridor, current camera and player control.

## 17. Performance implications

- One active signature situation maximum.
- Two to four bounded secondary loops maximum; core athlete/station cadence counts as one coordinated loop.
- One dominant animated surface maximum: the existing window/control plane.
- Reuse existing gym geometry, fluorescent form, three athletes, three stations and mechanical bars where feasible.
- No new dynamic light is artistically required; emissive or fixed-state treatment should carry the recalibration read.
- No reflection system, video surface, global simulation or scene-wide particle effect is required.
- Disable cue evaluation and media updates when EUX is inactive or off-screen according to the future technical contract.
- Mobile may use static display states and fewer transition samples while preserving the same narrative order.
- The accepted local scene ownership and deterministic reset remain the technical foundation; this contract does not authorize a generic engine.

## 18. DO NOT DO

- Athletes simply moving in a loop.
- Treadmills scrolling without narrative consequence.
- Decorative blue neon.
- Standard fitness KPI dashboards.
- A generic futuristic gym.
- A cyberpunk showroom.
- Hologram layers added as spectacle.
- A robot army or visibly robotic athletes.
- Permanent glitch effects.
- A floating dashboard or rain of numbers.
- A building that shakes instead of changing functional logic.
- Perfect synchronization without divergence, correction and residue.
- Simple acceleration at the signature/drop.
- Visual humor, slapstick correction or a gag object.
- Explicit dystopian branding, a written moral or explanatory narration.
- Industrial props that turn the metaphor into a literal assembly line.
- Assets or behaviors that could be copied unchanged to another track.

> **If an element could be moved unchanged to another track, it is not identity.**

## 19. Uniqueness contract

| Element | Function in EUX | Why it is unique here | Copiable elsewhere? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Three-role athlete system | Makes conformity, correction and residue readable through comparison | The title's gain/conformity exchange requires three distinct human responses to one imposed cadence | **No** — without A/B/C and the gym promise, the roles lose their meaning |
| Cadence stations | Convert voluntary exercise into production and correction | Ordinary sports equipment becomes the agent that trains the human and powers the room | **No** — unchanged reuse would repeat EUX's central inversion |
| Recalibration strip | Turns commercial hygiene lighting into a measuring act | It links body alignment, correction and storefront exposure in one physically motivated source | **No** — as a scanner elsewhere it would be a generic effect |
| Conformity window | Makes visible bodies simultaneously customers, products and measured units | Only EUX needs a storefront boundary that becomes the system's control surface | **No** — its function depends on public display of private effort |
| B correction | Gives the system a readable act of authority without dialogue | The station corrects a voluntary body in the language of optimization | **No** — it is the proof of the performance-production theme |
| C residue | Prevents a clean moral ending and preserves singularity as a small error | The remaining phase error answers “what is gained and what is lost” | **No** — its meaning depends on prior classification |
| Human freeze / machine continuation | Reverses the apparent owner of the activity | It completes the matrix anomaly: machines train people and move the room | **No** — it is EUX's signature situation |

The scene passes the uniqueness gate only when a viewer can describe the inversion, not merely identify a gym.

## Acceptance test

A valid implementation must let a tester answer, without explanatory text:

1. This is an ordinary gym displayed behind glass.
2. The bodies are being measured and corrected to serve a common cadence.
3. The machines continue for themselves and have become the real trainers.
4. One human difference survives long enough to leave doubt.

> **Do not decorate the gym. Reveal that it is not a gym.**
